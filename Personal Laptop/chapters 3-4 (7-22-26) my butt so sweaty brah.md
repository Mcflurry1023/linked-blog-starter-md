Lenses for Chapter 3 (Psych, Allegorical, Stylistic, Symbolic)
Psychological: Fear based compliance & Manipulation
	How does Squealer convince the animals that pigs deserve milk and apples

		The pigs require the food for health and not for carnal pleasure, but it contains important nutrients that pigs require. And they are above requiring of the food because they are "more knowledgeable" than the rest and that they are the ones taking care of the animals. He fear mongers saying Mr. Jones will return IF they are not given the food. "It is for YOUR sake that we drink that milk and eat those apples... Jones would come back!"
Cognitive Dissonance:
	Frames the pig's luxury as a sacrifice for all "It is for YOUR sake."
Fear Invoked:
	Threat of Mr. Jones' return as a control tactic.
Takeaway:
	Animals prioritize safety over questioning privileges.

Allegorical: The Working Class Under Dictatorship
	How do Boxer and the Sheep represent society's segments?
		Allegories are simple stories that can be used to talk about a much deeper and more serious topics 

		Boxer was a hard-worker, a symbol of the bolshevik's ideal proletariat worker. The sheep followed ideas without questioning it, the moment they learned "FOUR LEGS GOOD, TWO LEGS BAD", they repeated it over and over again.

Allegorical 2:
	Boxer being a loyal and hardworking proletariat makes him an idol for other animals, but he becomes very easily exploited to work.
		The sheep shows the unquestioned loyalty of the mass, they follow the rules without questioning it. The sheep repeats "FOUR LEGS GOOD, TWO LEGS BAD" without actually thinking about what it means for the other animals (birds and chickens), using it as a mechanism to drown out dissent and opposition.

Stylistic & Symbolic Lenses
	Stylistic:
		Orwell had a calm, matter-of-fact tone heightens dramatic irony
			He says it as if it doesn't matter much at all
			"The mystery of where the milk went was soon cleared up."
		The reader knows the pigs have betrayed the animals; the animals do not
			We are the omniscient viewers, privy to all information within the story
			Makes the theft much more disturbing
	Symbolic:
		Reveals how power corrupts Animalism
			Green Flag = the original and uncorrupted ideals of the rebellion.
			Oversimplified Maxims "Four legs good, Two legs bad" = reduced complex thought to propaganda. Oversimplification occurs for repeatability and recognizeability, removing the nuance of the original meaning and context of the bigger picture.

Chapters 4 (7-28-26)
**Battle of the Cowshed**
	The farm unites against the human attack, planting the early roots of pride and growing militarization.
		Key Themes: Foreign threat, Militarization, Patriotic distraction, War ethics
	Historical Lens - Russian Civil War (1918-1922)
		Red Army (Bolshevik) - associated with the animals
		White Army (Pro-Monarchists) - associated with the humans
			Loyalists of Tsar Nicholas wishing to put him back into power / Anti-communist elements.
			Pinchfield and Foxwood - The countries who supported the white army or at least were anti-communists
		Snowball  - Leon Trotsky
			Trotsky lead and defined the defense of the Bolshevik army
			Feats:
				Distracted and wore down the humans
				Faked a retreat to ambush the unprepared humans
				Snowball was injured with a shotgun and still fought back to Mr. Jones
	Structural Lens - Chapter 3 to Chapter 4
		Chapter 3 exposes the cracks in animalism
		Chapter 4 shows the continuous decline of the animals to the ideals of the humans
			May also show the active cover up by the pigs, right after the betrayal of the pigs. The other animals are distracted by the glorious victory of the battle.   **(DISTRACTION)**
		Structural Lens only to be used when it is specific to the text/literature
			- Unique Structure
			- Can be discussed as deliberate choices from the author
			Patriotism was used as a tool by the pigs
	Reader-Response & Symbolic Lenses
		Ethics of War & Militarization
		Boxer - "I have no wish to take life" shows moral sensitivity and remorse
		Snowball - "War is war" shows utilitarian pragmatism.
		Mr Jones' gun becomes a symbol of militarization, marking the farm's shift to armed defense
			Acts as a show of force, concept of MAD (Mutually Assured Distruction)
				"If you attack us, I will attack you, and we both lose"
			Displays militarization and power of the animals.
